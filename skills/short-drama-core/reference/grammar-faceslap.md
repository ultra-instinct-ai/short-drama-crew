# Grammar `faceslap` — 爽剧打脸

The payoff 短剧 audiences come for: the hero who was mocked, doubted or trampled **turns the
tables in public**. The whole beat is built for one feeling (扬眉吐气): the arrogance before, the
calm of the hero, the proof, then face after face changing its tune. Cut it as a sequence, not as
one shot. Combinations and exceptions: `grammar.md`.

Use it on a segment with `"grammar": "faceslap"` — always on the 爽点 of an episode whose
`satisfaction.type` is 打脸, 身份揭露, 智商碾压 or 复仇, and anywhere else the tables turn.

## 1. The sequence

| # | beat | cut | purpose | notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **the pressure** (压) | the villain's arrogance: the sneer, the insult, the crowd laughing along | `pressure` | the harder the setup, the harder the payoff; can sit in the segment before |
| 2 | **the calm** (静) | the hero unmoved: a small smile, a slow look up, the untouched tea | `performance` | stillness against their noise |
| 3 | **the entrance / the move** (起) | the hero stands, walks in, or someone important enters on their side; **low angle**, often slow motion | `reveal` / `decision` | the music enters here |
| 4 | **the proof** (证) | an insert on what turns the tables: the contract, the black card, the badge, the ring, the phone screen, the transfer receipt, the photograph | `evidence` / `reveal` | gated: every 打脸 shows its proof |
| 5 | **the cascade** (反应链) | face after face: the villain pales, the crowd whispers, the one who doubted drops their cup, the villain's ally steps away | `reaction` × 2 or more | gated: ≥ 2 reaction cuts (this segment and the next in the scene), on different people |
| 6 | **the landing** (落) | the villain's consequence (kneels, is escorted out, loses the deal) or the hero's quiet last line | `consequence` | the hook for the next beat can hide here |

Beats 1–2 may belong to the segment before. The gate checks 4 and 5; the advisory
`grammar-advice` notes a missing pressure cut, a missing low angle and a missing music hit.

## 2. Camera and framing

- **The hero from below** (`height: low`), centred, unhurried; the villain at eye level or from
  above as they shrink. Swap the power geometry of the earlier scenes.
- **The proof in close-up or extreme close-up**, held long enough to read (2–3 s). Readable text
  follows the prop's `textPolicy` (stage-art). A slap on the table is the classic entrance for it.
- **The cascade in close-ups**, each a different face, 2 s each, cut on the beat of the music.
  A `Push In` on the villain's face as it drains is the signature shot.
- **Slow motion** on the entrance or the proof (`in slow motion` in `motion`), once.
- **The crowd** is the chorus: one wide of the room changing its tune (heads turning, phones
  lowering) sells the scale. Staging per `maxOnScreen`.

## 3. Sound

`sound.music`: `silence` or `drop` under the insult (the room holds its breath), `enter` or
`swell` on the entrance or the proof, full through the cascade. `sound.sfx`: the slap of the
document on the table, the gasp, the cup falling, the murmur rising. A single line from the hero,
quiet, often lands harder than a speech.

## 4. With the other axes

| combination | how it plays |
| --- | --- |
| `low-key` look | the hero steps out of the shadow into the practical light for the reveal |
| `sweet-romance` look (霸总) | the male lead arrives to back her; the halo behind him on the entrance |
| `costume-dark` (宫斗) | the edict, the jade token, the imperial seal as the proof; kneeling as the landing |
| with `action` | the underdog's single decisive blow, then the cascade of the watching crowd |
| with `comedy` | the villain's reaction pushed to farce (the jaw drop, the spit take), still ≥ 2 reactions |

## 5. Example cut lines

- pressure: `the young heir laughs and tosses the contract onto the floor at her feet`
- entrance: `she rises from the chair in slow motion and looks up at him`
- proof: `she lays the black card on the table`
- reaction: `his smile drains away and he takes half a step back`
- reaction: `the manager's pen stops above the page`

## 6. Review questions

- Was the hero pushed down hard enough before?
- Can the audience read the proof?
- How many different faces change — at least two?
- Is the hero shot from below, and does the music come in?
