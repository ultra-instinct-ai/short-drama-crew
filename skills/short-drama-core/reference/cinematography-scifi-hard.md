# Cinematography preset `scifi-hard` — 硬科幻

The look: **the future as engineering.** Technology is functional, heavy and worn; every light has
a physical source (an instrument panel, a work lamp, a screen, an LED strip, the sun through a
viewport); in space, one harsh sun and black shadows. The palette is cold and neutral (white,
grey, gunmetal) with amber and red warning lights. The camera is sober, almost documentary, and
scale is real: a person is small next to a machine because the machine is big, not because it
glows. The mechanics shared by every preset are in `cinematography.md`.

## 1. Light — only what is built or natural — `light.source`, `light.direction`

Every cut names its source, carried verbatim in the frame with `grounded hard science fiction
lighting`. **No magic, floating holograms, force fields or energy glows** (gated: those sources are
refused). A display is a physical screen; a HUD is a reflection in a visor.

| place | the source | what it gives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ship corridor, lab | overhead panel strips, work lamps | hard top and side light, pools on the deck, dark between the panels |
| cockpit, control room | instrument panels, screens | cool light from below and the side on the faces, reflections in glass |
| spacewalk, surface | one harsh sun, the planet's glow | razor-edged shadows, black sky, a rim of earthlight |
| emergency | red and amber warning beacons | rotating hard colour, the face in and out of light |
| habitat | practical lamps, a window to the planet | warmer pools in a cold structure |

- The face is keyed **hard, from one side or from above** (`side-*`, `back-*`, `top`: at least half
  the cuts with people, gated). **Never** `frontal` or `ambient` on a person: space has no soft
  world light.
- Shadows are deep and hard-edged; contrast is high; fill comes only from bounce off a hull or a
  panel.
- **Scene lighting states** contain `grounded hard science fiction lighting` and the sources:
  > `grounded hard science fiction lighting: white overhead panel strips every two metres along the ship corridor, the deck between them in shadow, amber status lights glowing at each hatch, frost on the viewport at the far end. Empty corridor, no people.`

## 2. Design, colour and texture

- **Palette**: white, grey, gunmetal, bone, with amber and red for warnings and a single accent per
  faction or ship. Low saturation; the colour comes from the warning lights.
- **Design is functional**: cables, labels, stencilled numbers, scuffed paint, tape repairs,
  checklists. Make these the scene anchors and the foregrounds.
- **Wardrobe**: flight suits, pressure suits, work overalls with patches; helmets with visor
  reflections.
- **Physics as drama**: silence in vacuum (only the suit's breathing and radio), floating objects in
  zero-g, condensation of breath in a cold hull, the slow drift of debris.

## 3. Composition and camera

- **Scale by comparison**: a figure beside a machine, a ship against a planet, a suit on a
  hull. Wides and extreme wides establish it.
- **Symmetry of engineered spaces**: corridors, airlocks and control rooms are built symmetric;
  use them (`composition: art-first` pairs well).
- **Camera**: steady, observational: `Static Shot`, slow `Push In`, `Truck Left` / `Truck Right`
  along a corridor. Weightless float for zero-g (a slow drifting `Arc Shot`).
- **Lens**: 25–35mm for spaces, 50–85mm for faces behind visors, T2–T2.8.

## 4. Sound

Hums, fans, relays, alarms, radio chatter with squelch, breathing in a helmet, silence outside.
Music sparse: a low drone, a single piano line.

## 5. Review questions

- Could this be built? Is every light a panel, a lamp, a screen or the sun?
- Is there any floating hologram, glow or magic in the frame?
- Is the scale shown by comparison with something human?
- Is the palette cold and neutral, with colour only from warnings?
